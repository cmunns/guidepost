Positioning Guidepost

Six maintained competitors, measured from their published bundles rather than their documentation. The differentiator turns out to be narrower and more defensible than “built on native APIs” — and it is not bundle size.

Of seven tour libraries, Guidepost is the only one whose shipped code contains inert, the Popover API, CSS anchor positioning, or a reduced-motion rule.

Everyone else is still emulating in JavaScript what the browser now does natively, and the accessibility gaps are the visible consequence of that — not a separate failing. That single sentence is the position. Everything below is evidence for it.

Three claims you can defend

“The only tour library that contains the word inert.”

Evidence
Six competitors, zero uses. Every one of them either leaves background content reachable by Tab or fakes containment by intercepting keydown.
Why it lands
It is concrete, falsifiable in ten seconds, and maps directly onto WCAG 2.1.2 and 2.4.3 — the two criteria tours fail most often.
Caveat
Say “uses”, never “is the only accessible one”. React Joyride does real focus work without inert.

“No positioning engine. The browser keeps the card attached.”

Evidence
Shepherd and React Joyride ship Floating UI; Guidepost ships zero runtime dependencies and uses position-anchor, with a measured fallback.
Why it lands
Developers understand dependency weight instinctively. It is also the claim that ages into being true — anchor positioning support grows every quarter.
Caveat
Anchor positioning is not Baseline yet (Chrome 125+, Safari 26+, Firefox 147+). Lead with the fallback, never hide it.

“The animation respects prefers-reduced-motion. Nobody else’s does.”

Evidence
Not one competitor stylesheet contains the media query, and a spotlight sliding across the viewport is exactly the motion that triggers vestibular symptoms.
Why it lands
It is the detail that signals the whole library was built by someone who actually cares, rather than someone who added ARIA attributes at the end.
Caveat
Small on its own. Use it as the third beat, never the headline.

Claims to avoid

✗

“The smallest tour library.”

Driver.js is 7.9 kB to your 9.4 kB. Someone will check within the hour. Say “the smallest one that contains a focus contract” — still true, and the qualifier is the actual selling point.

✗

“WCAG 2.2 AA compliant.”

A library cannot be compliant; an integration can. Claim the specific mechanisms — focus enters and returns, background is inert, step changes announce, Escape always exits — and let the buyer map them to criteria.

✗

“Zero dependencies” as the headline.

Driver.js, Intro.js and TourGuide.js are all zero-dependency too. It is a supporting fact, not a wedge.

✗

“Shepherd is expensive, use this instead.”

Licence-cost framing invites the reply “then I will use Driver.js, which is also free.” Licence is the reason people start looking; accessibility is the reason they should pick you. Lead with the second.

Two audiences, two openings

Developers choosing a library

They arrive from a search, a README, or Hacker News, and they decide in about ninety seconds. Lead with the mechanism, not the benefit.

  • Hook: the browser now does the four hard parts; here is what is left.
  • Proof: the before/after table — Popper vs position-anchor, sentinel nodes vs inert.
  • Close: a live demo that reports which primitives their browser has.

Teams under an accessibility mandate

The European Accessibility Act became enforceable in June 2025, and EN 301 549 — which incorporates WCAG 2.1 AA in full — is the harmonised standard. Existing service contracts have until June 2027.

  • Hook: your onboarding tour is the most common accessibility regression in the product, because it is bolted on last.
  • Proof: the primitives matrix, plus the frame-by-frame test suite.
  • Close: a page mapping each mechanism to the criterion it serves.

README opening — the two paragraphs above the fold

Guidepost is a product-tour library with no positioning engine. The top layer, the tether, the focus containment and the exit animation are all things the browser now does natively, so what is left to write is the step machine and the accessibility contract — 9.4 kB of it, with no dependencies.

That is also why it is the only tour library whose shipped bundle contains inert. Focus containment is not a feature that was added; it is what you get when you stop reimplementing the platform.

Show HN opener

Show HN: Guidepost — a product-tour library built on inert, the Popover API and CSS anchor positioning

I needed a tour for a commercial app and found that the two best-known options are AGPL with paid commercial licences. Before writing another one I checked what the platform does now, and it turns out to be most of it. I also ran a probe over the six maintained alternatives: none of them use inert, and none respect prefers-reduced-motion. The comparison script is in the repo, so please check my work.

Risks, honestly

  1. Driver.js is smaller and has 26k stars. For anyone who does not care about accessibility, it wins. Do not fight on its ground; make accessibility the axis of comparison.
  2. The name may be taken on npm. Check before any launch post. A rename after the first thousand installs is far more expensive than one now.
  3. Anchor positioning is not Baseline. An honest support table on the landing page pre-empts the top Hacker News comment. Volunteering the limitation is worth more than the limitation costs.
  4. Zero adoption and one maintainer. Enterprise buyers read that as risk. Mitigate with the test suite, a changelog, and a written scope boundary — what this library will never try to do.
  5. The probe table is evidence, not an audit. If someone runs axe against a real Guidepost integration and finds violations, the whole position takes damage. Publish an axe run against the demo before the launch post, not after.

Method. Each library installed from npm in September 2026 and measured from its published artefacts: own bundle minified and gzipped with any shipped stylesheet included, runtime dependencies from package.json, primitives detected by regex over the bundle and stylesheet. Versions: driver.js 1.8.0, shepherd.js 15.3.0, intro.js 8.5.0, react-joyride 3.2.0, @reactour/tour 3.8.0, tourguidejs 1.1.5. Reproduce with node scripts/compare.mjs after installing the six as dev dependencies.

Not measured. Runtime performance, real screen-reader behaviour, and correctness of the ARIA that is present. A library can use aria-live badly. Three of the claims above would be stronger with an axe-core run behind them.
